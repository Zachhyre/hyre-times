Six-Figure Discounts Pile Up in Nashville
A 2020-built house bought for $670,000 in 2022 is now listed at $549,000, a $120,000 loss before fees.
By @nickgerli1
Nashville listing showing a $120,000 loss. Image via @nickgerli1 on X
Nick Gerli says six-figure price cuts are becoming common in Nashville, one of the most oversupplied metros in the country with a 56% inventory surplus. His example is a 2020 build in a trendy neighborhood whose owner has already cut $100,000 since July and is now listing about 10% below comps. With overbuilding and people moving out, even newer homes in good areas are dropping, and he thinks prices could fall further into 2027.
The CEO takeaway: oversupply hands buyers leverage and creates motivated sellers who bought near the peak. That's a real opening for acquisitions, but only if your offers reflect where prices are heading, not last year's comps, so check your buy-box assumptions against current inventory in every market you work.
